Diagnosing a Team's Operating System
1. Module Overview
Description
Learners apply structured diagnostics to identify the 2-3 highest-leverage issues affecting a real team — not every possible problem, but the handful that matter most.
Objective(s)
• Apply structured diagnostics to identify the 2-3 highest-leverage issues affecting a real team

2. Activity — Team OS Diagnostic Worksheet
Instructions: Use this worksheet to diagnose a real team's operating system — its agreements, roles, routines, and rituals, as they actually function, not as they're supposed to.
The team I'm diagnosing:
How does this team actually make decisions? (Not the stated process — what really happens.)
What roles and responsibilities are unclear or silently contested?
What routines exist, and which ones have quietly stopped serving their purpose?
Of everything above, what are the 2-3 highest-leverage issues — the ones that, if fixed, would meaningfully change how this team functions?
3. Assignment
Submit the completed diagnostic worksheet along with a short written explanation (150–200 words) of why you selected these specific issues as the highest-leverage ones, rather than others you observed.

Facilitating Agreement-Resetting Sessions
1. Module Overview
Description
Learners design and facilitate a session that resets a team's agreements and roles — including navigating real disagreement in the room, not avoiding it.
Objective(s)
• Design and facilitate a session that resets team agreements and roles, including through disagreement

2.2 Designing an Agreement-Resetting Session
Video + Worksheet
~8 min (as scripted)
2. Activity — Agreement-Resetting Session Design Worksheet
Instructions: Design a real session to reset agreements around one of the highest-leverage issues identified in Module 1.
The agreement or role issue this session will address:
How will I open the session so people feel safe naming real disagreement, not just going along?
What specific question or activity will surface the actual, current (not idealized) agreement?
How will I handle it if real disagreement surfaces in the room?
How will the session end — what will the team actually leave with?
3. Assignment
Facilitate the actual session with your team. Submit the completed design worksheet along with a brief (150-word) reflection on what happened, especially around any real disagreement that surfaced.

Introducing Routines & Rituals
1. Module Overview
Description
Learners select and introduce a new team ritual, anticipating real adoption barriers rather than assuming a good idea will simply stick on its own.
Objective(s)
• Select and introduce a new team ritual, anticipating real adoption barriers
Videos / Lessons

3.2 Designing and Introducing Your Ritual
Video + Worksheet
~8 min (as scripted)
2. Activity — Team Ritual Design Worksheet
Instructions: Design a new team ritual addressing one of the issues from your diagnosis, and think through how it will actually get adopted, not just introduced.
The ritual I'm introducing, and what it's meant to reinforce:
What's the simplest possible version of this ritual — something the team could realistically sustain?
What real barriers might get in the way of adoption? (Time pressure, skepticism, an existing competing habit.)
How will I introduce this so it doesn't feel like one more mandatory thing?
3. Assignment
Introduce the actual ritual to your team and run it for at least two cycles (e.g., two meetings, two weeks). Submit the completed worksheet along with a brief (150-word) reflection on whether it's actually being adopted, and what you're noticing.
4. Rubric
Criterion

Unsatisfactory
Fail

Some barriers considered but stay fairly generic
No real consideration of adoption barriers
Honest reflection on actual adoption
The reflection honestly reports what's actually happening, including any resistance or partial adoption
Some honest reporting but leans toward an optimistic gloss
No evidence the ritual was actually introduced and observed

2. Activity — Tracking Method & Retrospective Design Worksheet
Instructions: Design a simple way to track whether the agreement reset (Module 2) and ritual (Module 3) actually changed anything, then plan a real retrospective session.
What specific, observable signs would tell me the agreement reset actually took hold?
What specific, observable signs would tell me the ritual is actually being sustained?
How will I actually track these over the coming weeks? (Keep it simple enough to sustain.)
How will I structure the retrospective session — what questions will I ask the team?
3. Assignment
