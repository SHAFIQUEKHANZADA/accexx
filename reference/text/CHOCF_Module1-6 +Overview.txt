2. Activity — Change Impact Map Worksheet
Instructions: Choose one real, current change initiative and map its actual human impact, group by group.
The change initiative I'm mapping:
Group 1 affected — who are they, and specifically how does this change affect their day-to-day work?
Group 2 affected — who are they, and specifically how does this change affect their day-to-day work?
Group 3 affected — who are they, and specifically how does this change affect their day-to-day work?
Which group is likely to experience the greatest disruption, and why?

2. Activity — Resistance Pattern Worksheet
Instructions: Examine real or anticipated resistance from the highest-disruption group identified in Module 1.
The resistance I'm seeing or anticipating, and from whom:

3. Quiz
A short knowledge check (5 questions) on distinguishing resistance signal from resistance obstruction.
Sample Questions
• 1. An employee repeatedly asking detailed questions about a new system, rather than complying quietly, is most likely: (a) obstruction (b) a signal worth understanding (c) irrelevant
• 2. Treating all resistance as something to simply push through tends to: (a) resolve it quickly and permanently (b) drive it underground rather than resolve it (c) have no effect either way
• 3. The best first response to resistance is generally: (a) explain again, louder (b) understand what it's actually protecting (c) escalate immediately
• 4. True or False: Every instance of resistance reveals a legitimate concern that should change the plan.
• 5. A root-cause response addresses: (a) only the visible behavior (b) what's actually driving the behavior (c) whoever is most vocal
Followed by submission of the completed Resistance Pattern Worksheet above.

Designing Change Journeys with HOC
1. Module Overview
Description
Learners design a full change journey that accounts for beliefs and emotions, not just tasks and timelines, building on the impact map and resistance analysis from Modules 1–2.
Objective(s)
• Design a full change journey accounting for beliefs and emotions, not just tasks and timelines

Lesson
Format
Time

Video
~8 min (as scripted)
3.2 Designing Your Change Journey
Video + Worksheet
~8 min (as scripted)
2. Activity — Change Journey Design Worksheet
Instructions: Design a change journey for the group identified as highest-disruption in Module 1, accounting for the belief-emotion layer, not just the task timeline.
At the start of the change, what will this group likely believe, and feel?
What needs to happen, beyond tasks, for their beliefs to genuinely shift?
At the midpoint, what belief or emotional shift am I hoping to see, and how will I know?
By the end, what does genuine adoption — belief-level, not just compliance — actually look like?
3. Assignment
Submit the completed Change Journey Design along with a short explanation (150 words) of how this journey differs from a purely task-based project plan for the same change.
