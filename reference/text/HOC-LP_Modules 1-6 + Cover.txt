Human Operating Code™ Leadership Practitioner (HOC-LP)
Learn to diagnose and shift team behavior using the Human Operating Code framework — from everyday interventions to leading through conflict and change.
Format
Live Virtual or In-Person
Duration
42 contact hours
Facilitator
Dr. Laide R. Alexander — Forbes Coaches Council member, creator of the Human Operating Code framework, and author of the Amazon best-seller The Unfinished Leader. Alternatively, a professional executive assigned by Accexx Insight who meets this certification's stated facilitator qualifications.
CEUs
4.2 IACET CEUs (calculated at the standard 1 CEU: 10 contact-hours ratio)
Expected Outcome
Graduates can diagnose their own and their team's operating code, apply reframing and structured-conversation techniques to real performance and conflict situations, and execute a 90-day intervention plan for team culture.
Curriculum
Module 1: Introduction to the Human Operating Code (6 hrs.)
Learn the Beliefs-Stories-Emotions-Habits framework and use it to diagnose your own operating code.
Module 2: Diagnosing the Team's Operating Code (6 hrs.)
Apply structured diagnostic methods to identify the root cause behind a real team issue.
Module 3: Shifting Mindsets Through Structured Conversation (9 hrs.)
Practice reframing a limiting belief and planning a structured conversation for a real interaction.
Module 4: HOC in Performance & Feedback (6 hrs.)
Apply BSEH diagnostics to prepare and deliver feedback that addresses the belief behind the behavior.
Module 5: HOC in Conflict & Change (9 hrs.)
Use HOC-based de-escalation and reframing tools to work through a real conflict or resistance.
Module 6: Designing Everyday Interventions (6 hrs.)
Design a repeatable culture intervention and a 90-day implementation plan, defended to peers.

1. Module 1 Overview
Description
An introduction to the Beliefs–Stories–Emotions–Habits (BSEH) framework, the diagnostic lens behind every HOC-LP module. Learners see how invisible beliefs drive visible behavior, applying the lens to themselves before turning it toward others. They learn to diagnose and shift team behavior using the Human Operating Code framework — from everyday interventions to leading through conflict and change.
Objective(s)
• Explain the BSEH framework and how each element drives behavior
• Diagnose one's own operating code using a structured self-assessment

2. Activity — Diagnostic Interview Guide & Pattern-Statement Worksheet
Instructions: This worksheet has two parts. Part 1 structures how you gather information about a real team issue — either through honest self-reflection or an actual conversation with a colleague. Part 2 helps you turn what you found into a single, clear pattern statement.
Part 1: Diagnostic Interview Guide
The team issue I want to understand is:
What have I directly observed? (Specific incidents, not impressions.)
If I asked one team member honestly, what would they say is really going on? (Write what you believe they'd say, even if you haven't asked yet.)
What artifacts point to this issue? (Meeting notes, missed deadlines, emails, turnover, etc.)
Part 2: Pattern-Statement Worksheet
What's the surface-level symptom everyone would name?
Looking at everything above, what's the actual pattern underneath it — not the symptom, but what keeps producing it?
My one-sentence Team Pattern Statement:
3. Quiz
A short knowledge check (5 questions) distinguishing symptom-level statements from root-cause pattern statements.
Questions ((Note: these questions may change as the classes evolve)
• 1. "The team missed three deadlines this quarter" is an example of: (a) a root-cause pattern statement (b) a symptom (c) a belief
• 2. "The team avoids raising problems until it's too late because no one trusts that concerns will be acted on" is an example of: (a) a symptom (b) a root-cause pattern statement (c) an emotion
• 3. A well-written pattern statement should be: (a) a paragraph covering every possible cause (b) one clear sentence naming the underlying pattern (c) a list of all observed symptoms
• 4. Which is the best diagnostic source: (a) your own assumption about what's wrong (b) direct observation plus what team members would honestly say (c) what leadership already believes the problem is
• 5. True or False: A pattern statement should restate the symptom in more formal language.
Followed by submission of your own one-sentence Team Pattern Statement from the worksheet above.

Instructions: Use this worksheet to map a real, current conflict or instance of resistance you're facing — either with an individual or across a team.
