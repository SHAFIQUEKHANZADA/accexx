HR & People Systems, Organizational Culture & Strategy, and Education Institutions Consulting (24 Engagements)
Important framing: like coaching, consulting is a client engagement, not a course — the source Consulting Engagement Playbooks are explicit that there are no fixed modules, contact hours, or CEUs here. This listing helps a prospective client understand and select the right engagement; it is not a module catalog to enroll in one piece at a time. Every field below (objectives, process, tools, deliverables, qualifications, success measures) is extracted directly from the existing Consulting Engagement Playbooks — nothing here is newly invented.
None of these engagements are graded. Progress is reviewed against client-defined outcomes — validated findings, adopted roadmaps, implemented changes — never an external pass/fail standard.
HR & People Systems Consulting (6 Engagements)
Engagements that redesign the people-facing systems — hiring, onboarding, performance, recognition — that shape everyday culture.
HR Systems Audit & Redesign (HR-01)
Audits key people processes (hiring, onboarding, performance, recognition) for alignment with desired culture. Delivers a prioritized redesign roadmap.
Who It's For: HR leaders, People & Culture teams, organizations in growth or transformation.
Duration: Project-based, scoped to organization size — typically 6–10 weeks for a mid-sized organization.
Engagement Objectives
Audit key people processes for cultural alignment
Prioritize which processes need redesign first
Deliver a phased redesign roadmap
Engagement Process
Phase | Timing | What Happens
Discovery & Diagnosis | Weeks 1–3 | Stakeholder interviews, process mapping, and review of existing hiring, onboarding, performance, and recognition systems against the organization's stated culture.
Design | Weeks 4–6 | Co-design of redesigned process elements with the client's HR team, prioritized by impact and feasibility.
Delivery & Handoff | Weeks 7–8+ | Presentation of the prioritized redesign roadmap, brief training for the HR team on implementation, and a scheduled 60-day follow-up review.
Tools & Frameworks Used
Process-touchpoint mapping template
Cultural-alignment audit checklist (adapted from HCHR-D)
Impact/feasibility prioritization matrix
Deliverables
Current-state audit report
Prioritized redesign roadmap
Implementation toolkit for top-priority processes
Consultant Qualifications
Holds Accexx Insight's HCHR-D certification, or equivalent HR systems design experience (5+ years)
Experience conducting organizational audits
Success Measures
This engagement is not graded — success is defined by client adoption of the findings, not an external standard.
Client validates the audit findings as accurate
Redesign roadmap adopted into HR's actual work plan
A 60-day check-in confirms real implementation progress
Performance Management Transformation (HR-02)
Shifts performance management from fear-based appraisal to growth-centered conversations. Redesigns the system and trains managers to use it.
Who It's For: Organizations seeking to overhaul performance culture.
Duration: Project-based, typically 8–16 weeks.
Engagement Objectives
Diagnose the current performance-management approach for its fear/growth balance
Redesign the system toward growth-centered conversations
Train managers to use the redesigned system
Engagement Process
Phase | Timing | What Happens
Discovery & Diagnosis | Weeks 1–3 | Interviews or surveys on the current appraisal experience, and review of the existing system's structure and documentation.
Design & Pilot | Weeks 4–8 | Co-design of the redesigned performance-conversation structure and documentation, piloted with a small group of managers.
Manager Training & Rollout | Weeks 9–14 | Training managers on the redesigned approach (drawing on the HOC-LP performance & feedback module), with support through the first full cycle.
Handoff & Review | Weeks 15–16 | Review of pilot-cycle results, final adjustments, and handoff to HR for ongoing ownership.
Tools & Frameworks Used
Growth-centered performance system rubric (adapted from HCHR-D)
Manager training materials (adapted from the HOC-LP performance & feedback module)
Deliverables
Redesigned performance system documentation
Manager training materials
Pilot-cycle review report
Consultant Qualifications
Holds HCHR-D certification or equivalent, plus experience training managers in performance conversations
Ideally co-delivered with a HOC-LP certified facilitator for the training component
Success Measures
This engagement is not graded — success is defined by client adoption of the findings, not an external standard.
Manager confidence self-assessment before and after training
Qualitative feedback from the pilot cycle
Client's decision to continue the redesigned system after the pilot

Education Institutions Consulting (13 Engagements)
Engagements built specifically for K-12 and other education institutions — school brand, teaching quality, operations, and student experience.
School Brand & Reality Audit (EDU-01)
A 360° diagnostic of how leadership, teaching, systems, and parent relationships shape the school's market position. Delivers an honest assessment and 3–5 priority actions.
Who It's For: Private and public K-12 schools.
Duration: 3–4 hours (half-day) to multi-day, depending on scope.
Engagement Objectives
Assess how leadership, teaching quality, operations, and parent relationships actually shape the school's reputation
Identify the gap between the school's stated brand and families' lived experience
Deliver 3–5 honest, prioritized actions the school can realistically act on
Engagement Process
Phase | Timing | What Happens
Discovery | Pre-engagement | Review of enrollment data, family feedback (surveys, complaints, exit interviews if available), and current marketing materials.
On-Site Diagnostic | Half-day to 2 days | Stakeholder interviews (leadership, staff, and where possible, parents), classroom and front-office observation, and a facilitated leadership debrief.
Findings & Priorities | Within 1–2 weeks | Delivery of the honest assessment and 3–5 prioritized actions, presented to school leadership.
Tools & Frameworks Used
Brand-reality gap interview guide
Stakeholder observation checklist
Priority-action scoring matrix
Deliverables
Brand & reality audit report
3–5 prioritized action recommendations
Leadership debrief session
Consultant Qualifications
Holds EL-HOS certification or equivalent school leadership consulting experience (5+ years)
Experience conducting stakeholder interviews in a school setting
Success Measures
This engagement is not graded — success is defined by client adoption of the findings, not an external standard.
School leadership validates the audit findings as accurate, including uncomfortable ones
At least one priority action is adopted into the school's improvement plan
A 90-day check-in confirms real movement on the top priority

Accreditation & Quality Assurance Readiness (EDU-11)
Prepares leadership and faculty for accreditation reviews — aligning data, processes, quality assurance practices, and evidence collection.
Who It's For: Colleges and universities.
Duration: 3–4 hours (half-day) to multi-day, depending on scope.
Engagement Objectives
Assess current readiness against the relevant accreditation standard's evidence requirements
Align data collection, processes, and quality assurance practices to what reviewers will actually look for
Build a realistic pre-review action plan with owners and deadlines
Engagement Process
Phase | Timing | What Happens
Readiness Diagnostic | Pre-engagement to half-day | Review of existing evidence, data practices, and prior review findings (if any) against the accreditation standard's requirements.
Gap Closure Planning | Half-day to 1 day | Facilitated session to prioritize gaps and assign ownership for evidence collection and process alignment.
Pre-Review Support | Ongoing, scoped separately | Optional continued support as the institution works through its action plan ahead of the actual review.
Tools & Frameworks Used
Accreditation evidence-readiness checklist
Gap-closure action plan template
Deliverables
Readiness diagnostic report
Prioritized gap-closure action plan with owners and deadlines
Facilitated planning session
Consultant Qualifications
Direct experience with accreditation review processes (as reviewer, preparer, or consultant)
Familiarity with the specific accrediting body relevant to the client
Success Measures
This engagement is not graded — success is defined by client adoption of the findings, not an external standard.
Leadership validates the diagnostic as an accurate picture of current readiness
The action plan is adopted with real deadlines, not left as a reference document
The institution reports measurable readiness improvement ahead of its actual review date

Student Experience & Support Systems Mapping (EDU-13)
Maps the full student journey from admission to graduation, highlighting pain points. Clarifies roles across academic advising, student affairs, counselling, and faculty.
Who It's For: Colleges and universities.
Duration: 1 day (6 hours).
Engagement Objectives
Map the full student journey from admission through graduation
Identify the specific points where students experience the most friction or fall through the cracks
Clarify roles and handoffs across advising, student affairs, counseling, and faculty
Engagement Process
Phase | Timing | What Happens
Journey Mapping | Half-day | Facilitated mapping session with representatives from advising, student affairs, counseling, and faculty to build the full student journey map.
Pain Point & Role Clarification | Half-day | Identification of the highest-impact pain points and clarification of roles and handoffs at each stage.
Map Handoff | Within 1 week | Delivery of the finalized journey map and role-clarity document.
Tools & Frameworks Used
Student journey mapping template
Cross-functional role and handoff matrix
Deliverables
Full student journey map
Prioritized pain-point analysis
Cross-functional role and handoff clarity document
Consultant Qualifications
Experience in student affairs, academic advising, or institutional effectiveness
Comfortable facilitating cross-departmental mapping sessions with competing perspectives
Success Measures
This engagement is not graded — success is defined by client adoption of the findings, not an external standard.
Representatives from all involved departments validate the map as accurate
At least one identified pain point is assigned an owner and an improvement plan
Role clarity at key handoff points is confirmed by both sides of the handoff (e.g., advising and student affairs)
