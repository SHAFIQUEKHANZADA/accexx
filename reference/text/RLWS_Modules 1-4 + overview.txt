Diagnosing Organizational Drivers of Burnout
1. Module Overview
Description
Learners use a structured diagnostic to assess three real organizational drivers of burnout — workload, control, and fairness — and identify the highest-risk areas on a real team.
Objective(s)
• Use a structured diagnostic to assess workload, control, and fairness
• Identify the highest-risk areas for burnout on a real team
Videos / Lessons
Lesson
Format
Time

Video
~8 min (as scripted)
2.2 Diagnosing Your Team's Highest-Risk Areas
Video + Worksheet
~8 min (as scripted)
2. Activity — Burnout Driver Diagnostic Worksheet
Instructions: Assess a real team across three drivers of burnout, using specific evidence rather than general impressions.
Workload — is the actual volume and pace sustainable? What's the evidence?
Control — do people have real say over how they do their work, or is it dictated with no input?
Fairness — are workload, recognition, and opportunity distributed in ways people perceive as fair?
Of these three, which represents the highest-risk area for this team right now, and why?
3. Quiz
A short knowledge check (5 questions) on the real organizational drivers of burnout.
Sample Questions
• 1. Which of the following is a genuine organizational driver of burnout, distinct from individual resilience: (a) an employee's personal coping skills (b) a chronic lack of control over how work gets done (c) an employee's personality
• 2. "Control," in this diagnostic, refers to: (a) how strictly a manager supervises (b) how much real say people have over how they do their work (c) how many rules exist
• 3. "Fairness" as a burnout driver is mainly about: (a) treating everyone identically regardless of role (b) whether workload, recognition, and opportunity are perceived as equitably distributed (c) following all company policies exactly
• 4. True or False: High workload alone, with real control and fairness present, is always the primary driver of burnout.
• 5. The best evidence for a burnout diagnostic is: (a) general impressions of team mood (b) specific, real evidence tied to workload, control, and fairness (c) how the team compares to other companies
Followed by submission of the completed Burnout Driver Diagnostic Worksheet above.

2. Activity — Healthy Norms Commitment & Embedding Plan
Instructions: Identify one real, unhealthy norm you may be modeling as a leader, and commit to a specific alternative.
An unhealthy norm I might currently be modeling, even unintentionally (e.g., late-night emails, never taking real breaks, working through illness):
What does this norm actually teach my team, regardless of what I say I value?
The specific, healthier norm I'm committing to model instead:
How will my team actually notice this change? What will be visibly different?
3. Assignment
Submit the completed commitment worksheet as your final deliverable for the self-paced RLWS track, along with a short explanation (150 words) of why this particular norm matters most to shift right now.
