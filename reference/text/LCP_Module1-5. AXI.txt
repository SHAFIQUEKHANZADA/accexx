Leader-as-Coach Practitioner (LCP)
Build a coaching-style leadership practice — powerful questioning, and coaching through performance conversations, change, and resistance.
Format
Live Virtual or In-Person
Duration
30 contact hours

Dr. Laide R. Alexander — Forbes Coaches Council member, creator of the Human Operating Code framework, and author of the Amazon best-seller The Unfinished Leader. Alternatively, a professional executive assigned by Accexx Insight who meets this certification's stated facilitator qualifications.

Graduates can shift fluidly between coaching, directing, and supporting — applying real coaching skills to performance conversations and change situations, demonstrated in an observed coaching session.

Module 1: Foundations of Coaching-Style Leadership (6 hrs)

Apply a decision framework to choose the right stance, tested in a final observed coaching session.

2. Activity — Coaching Stance Self-Assessment Worksheet
Instructions: Use this worksheet to examine your own default leadership stance — where you naturally default to directing or mentoring when coaching might actually serve better.
A recent, real conversation where I directed (told someone what to do):
Looking back, would a coaching approach — asking rather than telling — have served better here? Why or why not?
A recent, real conversation where I mentored (shared my own experience/advice):
What's my honest default stance under pressure — coaching, directing, or mentoring — and what does that cost the people I lead?

Submit the completed worksheet along with a short written reflection (150–200 words) on your honest default stance and one real situation where shifting toward coaching could change the outcome.

3. Quiz
A short knowledge check (5 questions) on distinguishing closed from open questions and identifying listening levels.
Sample Questions
• 1. "Did that frustrate you?" is an example of: (a) an open question (b) a closed question (c) a values-level question
• 2. "What was hardest about that for you?" is an example of: (a) a closed question (b) an open question (c) a directive statement
• 3. Noticing someone's voice tighten while they describe a project is listening at the: (a) content level (b) emotion level (c) values level
• 4. "Why does getting this right matter so much to you?" is aimed at the: (a) content level (b) emotion level (c) values level
• 5. True or False: A question that starts with "Don't you think..." is genuinely open.

2. Activity — Change Resistance Coaching Worksheet
Instructions: Think of a real team member currently showing resistance to a change you're leading. Use this worksheet to plan a coaching-style conversation rather than a push-through approach.
The change, and the resistance I'm seeing:
If I treated this resistance purely as an obstacle, what would I likely do? (Your default response.)
What might this resistance be signaling — what's it possibly protecting?
An open question I could ask to understand the signal, instead of pushing past it:
How will I know the difference between resistance that's worth exploring and resistance I still need to move through?
3. Assignment
Have the actual conversation with the team member. Submit the completed worksheet along with a brief (150-word) reflection on what you learned about the resistance once you actually asked, rather than assumed.
