2. Activity — Ethical Drift Self-Audit & Safeguards Worksheet
Instructions: Honestly examine a real pattern of small compromises in your own leadership, and design a real safeguard against further drift.
A small compromise I've made more than once, that felt minor each time:
Looking at the pattern honestly, not just the individual instances — where is this actually heading if it continues?
What made each individual instance feel justified in the moment?
A real, specific safeguard I'm putting in place to catch this earlier next time:
3. Quiz
A short knowledge check (5 questions) on how ethical drift actually happens.
Sample Questions
• 1. Ethical drift typically happens through: (a) one large, obvious violation (b) a series of small compromises that each feel individually justified (c) random chance
• 2. Each individual small compromise usually feels: (a) clearly wrong at the time (b) justified given the specific circumstances (c) irrelevant
• 3. The best way to catch ethical drift is to look at: (a) any single instance in isolation (b) the pattern across multiple instances over time (c) only the most recent instance
• 4. True or False: A safeguard against ethical drift should be reactive, addressed only after a serious violation occurs.
• 5. A genuine personal safeguard is: (a) a vague intention to "do better" (b) a specific, real mechanism that catches the pattern early (c) not necessary if someone has good intentions
Followed by submission of the completed Ethical Drift Self-Audit & Safeguards Worksheet above.
